Solution

The problem with traditional pesticides is that the chemicals used can endanger the surrounding environment. This can cause serious damage to foliage and animal life in the environment leading to some having to leave their habitat as it is no longer safe. But know this: when it comes to us, we can assure you that our methods of pest extermination won’t harm the environment in any manner. We are able to achieve this by using pesticides that can naturally be found in the environment. These pesticides are able to exterminate pests and deter rodents from entering your home, all while being as environmentally safe as possible.

Diatomaceous Earth is a white crumbly powder made up of tiny fossilized shells of water algae known as diatoms and can be found in dry lake beds. Diatomaceous Earth works by drying out insects and absorbing their oils and fats from the cuticle of their exoskeletons. Diatomaceous Earth, if eaten or absorbed through skin contact by a human, has minimal effects and can be excreted from the body. However, if it is inhaled it can increase the risk of lung disease. That is why, if we must use this product, we ask that you remain away from the treated area until we let you know it is safe to return.

Pyrethrin is a pesticide naturally found in chrysanthemum flowers and is made up of six chemicals that are toxic to insects. Pyrethrin works by overstimulating an insect's nervous system after it is touched or ingested, leading to paralysis and eventually death. Although Pyrethrin has low toxicity for humans, inhaling it can irritate the respiratory system and may cause coughing or diarrhea. If absorbed through skin contact, it is broken down and removed from the body with only mild irritation. Proper ventilation helps reduce exposure after application.

Boric acid and sodium borate salts are pesticides naturally found in soil, plants, and water. Boric acid is effective against spiders, fungi, and mould. It works by either being eaten by pests, disrupting their stomach and nervous system, or by damaging the outer layer of an insect's exoskeleton. Because it can also affect plant life, we only use it in controlled areas where there is little to no risk of spillage. If inhaled or swallowed, Boric acid can still cause symptoms such as nausea, diarrhea, and vomiting. Boric acid as a pesticide is only to be used indoors.

By using these naturally occurring chemicals, we are able to reduce environmental harm while still effectively controlling pests. This is only the first step, and we intend to implement even more environmentally friendly practices in the future. However, please note that children are generally more sensitive to pesticides and can be harmed more easily than adults.
